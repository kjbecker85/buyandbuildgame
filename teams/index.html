Teach a whole deal in one sitting

Buy and Build is a strategy game about private equity. Each player runs a $50 million fund: buys a company with a loan, buys add-ons, reads the statements every quarter, and chooses when to sell. Your group plays at the same time, and a private leaderboard ranks the room.

Who it is for

One session takes 60 to 90 minutes. It needs a browser and nothing else.

Firms and search funds

New analysts and associates see the whole life of a deal in one afternoon: the price, the loan, the add-ons, the exit, and what each did to the return.

Business school classes

For courses in entrepreneurship, private equity, mergers and acquisitions, and corporate finance. Students make the decisions before they study them.

Workshops and events

A room plays at once on laptops and phones. The leaderboard goes on the big screen, and the debrief turns the results into lessons.

What players practice

Every number in the game comes from a full set of financial statements that the player can open at any time.

  • The price and the loanHow much to pay, how much to borrow, and what the bank does when profit falls short.
  • Buy and buildBuying smaller companies at a low multiple and selling the larger company at a higher one.
  • The three statementsProfit and loss, cash flow and the balance sheet, quarter by quarter, with a prediction to make on each.
  • Running the companyPrices, contracts, a management team, suppliers and collections. Each move pays less when it is overused.
  • The exitBuyers make offers every quarter in a market that rises and falls. The player chooses the moment to sell.
  • The score investors useFund value against a line that grows 8 percent a year, and the rank among three funds.

How a session runs

You can run it yourself with the session guide, or ask for a pilot with a live debrief.

  1. Make a groupIn the game, make a group and get a group code and a link. It takes a minute.
  2. Players joinThey open the link on a laptop or a phone and make an account. There is nothing to install.
  3. Everyone plays a quick dealOne company, about 25 minutes, against two rival funds.
  4. The leaderboard ranks the roomOnly your group can see it. It shows each player's best fund value since the group was made.
  5. DebriefCompare the best and the worst results: the loan, the add-ons, the timing of the sale.
