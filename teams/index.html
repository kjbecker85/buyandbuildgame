Teach a whole deal in one sitting

Buy and Build is a free strategy game about private equity that plays in a browser. Each player runs a $50 million fund. They buy a company with a bank loan, grow it by buying smaller companies called add-ons, read its financial statements every quarter, and choose when to sell. Your group plays at the same time, and a leaderboard that only your group can see ranks the players.

Who it is for

A session takes 60 to 90 minutes. Everyone plays one quick deal, then the group holds a debrief, a guided discussion of what the players did and why. Players need only a browser and a free account.

Firms and search funds

New analysts and associates see the whole life of a deal in one afternoon. They set the price and the loan, buy add-ons and sell the company, then see how much each of those choices added to the return.

Business school classes

It fits courses in entrepreneurship, private equity, mergers and acquisitions, and corporate finance. Students make a deal's decisions themselves before they study them, so the lecture can start from choices they made.

Workshops and events

Everyone in the room plays at once on laptops and phones. You put the group's leaderboard on the big screen, and the debrief draws the lessons out of the results.

What players practice

Every number in the game comes from a full set of financial statements for the company. Players open more of the statements as their career in the game grows.

  • The price and the loanHow much to pay for a company, how much to borrow, and what the bank does when profit falls too low to support the loan.
  • Buy and buildThe strategy the game is named for. A multiple is the price of a company divided by its yearly profit. Players buy small companies at about 5 times profit, combine them, and sell the larger company at a higher multiple.
  • The three statementsThe profit and loss statement, the cash flow statement and the balance sheet, quarter by quarter. In some quarters the player predicts one number on them before the quarter is played.
  • Running the companyRaising prices, selling contracts, hiring a management team, and dealing with suppliers and with customers who pay late. Each move pays best the first time, less the second time, and costs sales when it is overused.
  • The exitFrom Year 4 to Year 7 buyers make offers every quarter, and the price they offer rises and falls with the market. The player chooses when to sell.
  • The score investors useFund value is the fund's unspent money plus what its company would sell for today, less the loan. It must finish above the investors' line, the 8 percent a year that investors expect. Then the three funds are ranked by fund value.

How a session runs

There are three ways to run it: on your own with the free session guide, with a classroom license, or as a pilot in which Kyle Becker leads the debrief live.

  1. Make a groupIn the game, you make a group and get a group code and a link to share. It takes about a minute.
  2. Players joinPlayers open the link on a laptop or a phone and make a free account, so that their scores reach the group's leaderboard. There is nothing to install.
  3. Everyone plays a quick dealEach player buys one company, grows it and sells it, against two rival funds played by the computer. It takes about 25 minutes.
  4. The leaderboard ranks the roomOnly your group can see it. It shows each player's best fund value from the games finished since the group was made.
  5. DebriefCompare the best and the worst results, and ask what made the difference: the size of the loan, the add-ons bought, or when the company was sold.
